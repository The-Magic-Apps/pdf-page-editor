Frequently asked

How do I open the editor?

Click the PDF Page Editor icon in Edge’s toolbar. If you can’t see it, open the Extensions menu (the jigsaw piece) and choose the eye icon next to PDF Page Editor to show it in the toolbar. The editor opens in its own tab.

How do I combine or split PDFs?

Open your PDFs in the lower pane, by dropping them there or with Choose files. Each gets a tab. Then build the new document in the upper pane: drag pages up, double-click them, or use Add all, Add selected or Add range. Arrange, rotate and flip pages in the upper pane, then choose Save PDF.

Are my files uploaded?

No. Everything happens inside Edge on your computer, and the extension makes no network requests. See the privacy policy.

Can I open a password-protected PDF?

Yes. You will be asked for its password, which is kept only while the file is open. Pages you save from it are not password-protected.

Why won’t a file open?

The editor opens PDF files up to 500 MB. A file that is damaged, or that isn’t really a PDF, can’t be opened; you will see a message saying which.

What is kept in the PDF I save?

Everything on the pages: text (still selectable and searchable), pictures and drawings, and links and comments placed on a page. Not kept: bookmarks, links that jump between pages, and digital signatures (any change to a signed PDF invalidates its signature). Fillable form fields may stop being fillable.

Can I edit the text on a page?

No. PDF Page Editor arranges whole pages; it doesn’t change what is on them.

Are there keyboard shortcuts?

Yes, for everything. Press ? in the editor to see them all. For example, R rotates the selected pages, Delete removes them, and Ctrl+S (⌘S on a Mac) saves.

Can I use it in Google Chrome?

It is published for Microsoft Edge. It is built on the same extension platform as Chrome, but we only test and support it in Edge for now.

Reporting bugs

Please include:

  • The extension’s version (shown on its card at edge://extensions)
  • Your Edge version (edge://settings/help) and operating system
  • The steps that led to the problem
  • What you expected to happen, and what actually happened
